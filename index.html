How “RTK II” and EGFR status change the picture

RTK II is a DNA-methylation class of IDH-wildtype glioblastoma, sitting in the EGFR / “classical” family. These tumours very often carry EGFR gene amplification, sometimes the EGFRvIII rearrangement or an EGFR point mutation, usually with CDKN2A/B loss and chromosome 7 gain / chromosome 10 loss.

Almost no trial enrols by methylation class. What actually drives trial matching:

  • The exact EGFR finding. Trials differ sharply: some accept amplification alone (ABBV-637/ERAS-801, ERAS-801-SARG, intra-arterial cetuximab), some require EGFRvIII specifically (BRiTE), and one requires a pathogenic point mutation or EGFRvIII and explicitly excludes amplification-only tumours (WSD0922-FU). Ask whether EGFRvIII was actually tested — it is not on every standard NGS panel.
  • MGMT promoter methylation status. RTK II tumours are frequently MGMT-unmethylated, which opens several trials designed specifically for unmethylated disease. This list has already removed the 6 trials that require MGMT-unmethylated status for this case — if that changes, re-add them from the full dataset.
  • Tissue that was banked. The personalised-vaccine trials (DOC1021, AV-GBM-1, Bvax, BreakVax) need tumour material from the resection — some need viable cells or fresh-frozen tissue, not just a paraffin block. Use the Tissue / specimen needed filter above to see exactly which, and confirm what was saved from the 1.2 cm specimen with the surgical/pathology team.

Reality check on the class: EGFR-targeted drugs have repeatedly failed in unselected glioblastoma (systemic cetuximab; the depatuxizumab-mafodotin antibody-drug conjugate in the randomised INTELLANCE-1 trial). Checkpoint inhibitors alone were negative in newly diagnosed disease (CheckMate-498, CheckMate-548). The current trials bet on brain-penetrant drugs, better patient selection, and combinations. No immunotherapy or EGFR-targeted approach is yet standard of care for newly diagnosed glioblastoma.

Private providers — cell manufacturing outside a clinical trial

Clinics that manufacture a personalised cell product — a dendritic-cell vaccine made from the patient’s own tumour tissue, or expanded/activated NK and T cells — and sell it directly to patients, generally outside any registered clinical trial. This is a fundamentally different category from every trial above: not FDA- or EMA-approved, not randomised, evidence is almost always a small case series or none at all, and it is paid for out of pocket (commonly USD/EUR tens of thousands). Included for completeness because they were asked for — not as a recommendation.

Read this before contacting any of these. Glossary

IDH-wildtype
No mutation in the IDH1/IDH2 genes. Defines the most aggressive adult glioblastoma group; given here.
Methylation class / RTK II
Tumours grouped by genome-wide DNA-methylation pattern. “GBM, RTK II” is the EGFR / classical subgroup.
MGMT promoter methylation
Silencing of a DNA-repair gene. Methylated = better temozolomide response; unmethylated = less benefit, common in RTK II.
EGFR amplification
Many extra copies of the EGFR gene. The hallmark lesion of RTK II GBM.
EGFRvIII
A specific EGFR rearrangement producing a tumour-specific protein; a target for several CAR-T and bispecific trials. Not always tested by default.
GTR
Gross-total resection — removal of essentially all visible enhancing tumour.
KPS / ECOG
Performance-status scales. KPS 100→0; ECOG 0→5. Most trials need KPS ≥60–70 or ECOG 0–2.
RANO / RANO 2.0
Standardised MRI-based criteria for scoring glioma response and progression.
DLT
Dose-limiting toxicity — a pre-defined severe side effect used to set the dose in early-phase trials.
Checkpoint inhibitor
Antibody that releases a brake on T cells (anti–PD-1, anti–PD-L1, anti–CTLA-4, anti–LAG-3).
CAR-T
A patient’s T cells re-engineered to recognise a tumour antigen (e.g. EGFRvIII, B7-H3, IL13Rα2).
Bispecific / BiTE
An antibody with two arms — one grabs a tumour antigen, one grabs a T cell — forcing them together.
Dendritic-cell vaccine
The patient’s antigen-presenting cells, loaded with tumour material, re-infused to prime an anti-tumour response.
RNA-LP / mRNA vaccine
Tumour mRNA packaged in lipid particles to teach the immune system the tumour’s antigens.
CED
Convection-enhanced delivery — slow catheter infusion of a drug directly into brain tissue, bypassing the blood-brain barrier.
BBB opening (focused ultrasound)
Ultrasound + microbubbles that transiently loosen the blood-brain barrier so systemic drugs reach the tumour.
TTFields
Tumour Treating Fields (Optune) — wearable alternating electric fields, part of standard maintenance care.
Pseudoprogression
Treatment-related MRI changes that mimic tumour growth, common after chemoradiation; can affect trial eligibility.
