These are included, toggleable, because they were specifically asked about — not as a recommendation. Discuss any of them with the treating team before use.

      How the optimization score works

      Fully transparent, on purpose — expand any item's detail panel to see exactly which category, evidence grade, and interactions are feeding the number.

      1. Each category has a fixed weight (they sum to 100), split evenly across every item assigned to it, whether or not that item is currently switched on. Turning on more items in a category doesn't inflate the score — the category's ceiling stays the same.

      2. Each active item's evidence grade scales its contribution the same way this whole site's evidence score works elsewhere:

      EvidenceContribution factorMeaning
      5/5×1.00Positive randomised phase 3
      4/5×0.85Positive randomised phase 2
      3/5×0.65Single-arm survival signal vs. historical control
      2/5×0.45Early/observational signal, survival immature
      1/5×0.25First-in-human or mechanistic only, no outcomes yet
      Varies / not scored×0.40Protocol not yet confirmed (e.g. the Duke trial)
      ⚠ Caution×−0.30A trial of this specific approach was negative, or no human data exists at all for something with real risk

      3. Interactions between active items adjust the total directly — a conflict (e.g. oral antioxidant vitamin C switched on during concurrent chemoradiation) subtracts points and raises a warning; a synergy (e.g. steroid-sparing switched on alongside the Duke trial) adds points. These only fire when both items in the pair are active, regardless of which phase you're looking at.

      What this is not: a survival estimate, a probability, or a ranking of "better" vs. "worse" medicine in any absolute sense. It is a way to see, transparently, how a chosen combination stacks up against the literature gathered here — nothing more.
